# Historical B1 architecture: English, Spanish and French

October editorial source; recording and production decisions did not carry forward into November. Source receipt SHA-256: 4edc28ddc0a96aeefd838a721d99164ef8c7d83f0c4a727da94554d25445bde8.

The German B1 engine can carry English, Spanish and French without new lesson mechanics. Three pieces are German-shaped and must be generalized first: the register type, the B1 validator, and the language tables in the two TTS scripts. The German device gate covers the interaction contract. It does not cover accents and elision in typed cloze blanks, two-turn speech recognition in the three locales, or the base-edition and catalog coupling that did not exist when German B1 shipped in July. Nothing below claims approval for production SQL, edition regeneration or ElevenLabs spend. The board shows Codex holding an in-flight claim on this same scope today, so this stays a read-only spec.

## Milestone 1 and its gates

Milestone 1 is three verified B1 pilots. For each language: a scaffold module, a full spec with the 100-trophy pre-allocation, a validator config, and Practical 1 complete, with L1 to L3 handwritten by Fable as exemplars and L4 to L10 drafted by Codex, reviewed, integrated, suites green. It ends with a held local commit, not a push. Unlike German in July, any new authored base text now changes the language's corpus hash, so its twelve explanation editions stop validating and learners on other UI locales hit the recovery state. The phrase catalog test also demands a byte-identical committed migration. Both steps are paid or owner-gated and are worth doing once per language after the full tier, not per pilot path.

Sequence per language, parallel across languages, sequential within one:

1. **Engine prerequisites, one shared commit.** Widen the register type in `guidedLessons.ts` to also allow usted, tú, vous, tu, formal and casual. Only the resolver copies this field and only the validator reads it, so the change is type-only.
2. **Validator generalization.** Refactor `validate-guided-draft-b1.ts` from German constants into a per-language config block on the A2 phase-2 pattern: target language, path prefix, speak locale, base locales, recall variant, space before punctuation, register pair with its mid-sentence checks, learner-turn rails, and a staged-anchor table. The current file filters German, asserts exactly 100 lessons and hardcodes de-DE.
3. **TTS tables.** Add the slugs to the B1 language guards in `seed_guided_bright_rotation.py` and the batch runner. The surface constraint migration for dialogue and pattern is already applied, so no schema work.
4. **Suite pins.** Add per-language B1 path-id lists and episode-field asserts to `test-guided-today-data.ts`, and new rows to the trophy matrix's B1 block. Update counts deliberately, never loosen.
5. **Scaffolds** from `germanB1.ts`, not from the A2 modules: compact input with register, dialogue, pattern and cloze; estimated minutes 7, speak 15 seconds at threshold 0.8; locales es-ES, fr-FR, en-US; ids `{lang}-b1-practical-{n}-{NNN}-{slug}`; path metadata level B1, base language German. Copy each A2 module's accepted-answer helper, Spanish and French accent-stripping and English plain, and apply it to cloze blanks and lesson items alike, because the runtime matcher is exact after NFC and lowercasing. Base locales follow each language's own corpus: Spanish and French carry de and en, English carries de only plus the bilingual situation. Song genre strings continue the A2 modules. Pattern labels use the target language's grammar term.
6. **Specs** on the German skeleton, with the 100-trophy pre-allocation generated by module import, never by text search. English A1 builds trophies through a helper call, so a grep misses all 271 A1 words.
7. **Pilot drafting**: Fable writes P1 L1 to L3, Codex drafts L4 to L10 at xhigh under the B1 batch contract, then validator, Fable read-through, adversarial review, arbitration.
8. **Device gate, scoped.** The German gate on 2026-07-17 ran on desktop with the owner's voice feedback; the iPhone pass is still deferred on the board. It proved cloze focus, chip fallback, two-part role-play and the build cue, and that contract is reusable. Per new language the owner plays one P1 lesson on the phone and checks typed accents and ñ, French apostrophes and the space before ? and !, and that both role-play turns pass in that locale's recognizer. The owner cannot gate nativeness here. The adversarial review and Fable's read carry it, and the independent review the rules require for learner-facing content applies.

Milestone 2 is P2 to P10 per language in three Codex batches of three paths, same gates. Milestone 3 per language is the release bundle: twelve editions regenerated, catalog and locale migrations with rollback tests, TTS batch, pins, scoped commit, push.

## Anchor and staging spec

The shared spine and shapes stay: P1 B, P2 B, P3 A, P4 C, P5 A, P6 A, P7 C, P8 B, P9 A, P10 B. Anchors come from each language's own B1 inventory, PCIC B1, DELF B1 with CEFR-CV, and Cambridge B1 Preliminary with the English Grammar Profile. P5 settings are each country's rite of passage, not the Bürgeramt.

**Register values.** Spanish usted or tú, French vous or tu. Both A1 and A2 corpora are formal-only, verified by search, so B1 introduces the informal form as a deliberate upgrade: the Local has friends. English has no T/V distinction; formal or casual drives tone rails only. Formal bans yeah, gonna, wanna, hey and you guys; casual bans sir and ma'am. Distribution as German: P5, P6 and P9 mostly formal; P2, P7, P8 and P10 mostly informal; P1, P3 and P4 mixed. One interlocutor per lesson, singular only, so vosotros, ustedes and plural vous never arise.

**Gender safety.** Spanish stays fully gender-free. Indefinido, imperfecto, condicional and subjuntivo forms carry no gender. Ban speaker adjectives and participles such as cansado, perdido, preocupado, nervioso, contento, solo, niño and pequeño, with recasts: tenía sueño, tenía prisa, me alegro, cuando tenía diez años, de joven, cuando vivía en. French cannot narrate without être verbs, so the default is the Polish and Russian precedent. The French roster is Lilly, Adam, Stephyra, Guilamme and the B1 rotation offset is 20, so odd paths get a female voice and even paths a male voice; verify the genders in the voices table. Written être and reflexive participles in learner lines follow the path's voice, accepted answers carry both forms, and such participles are never a recall answer, speak token or trophy. The alternative is the A2 avoir-only ban with avoir recasts. Speaker adjectives stay banned in French. English has no issue.

Rules every spec restates: them₂ is never foreshadowed in the scene. Typed blanks never take bare pronouns or articles and never span an apostrophe or elision; French and English keep the apostrophe in the text segment. Multi-word answers stay within three words. Choice blanks appear only where typing would test spelling. Each blank ships four same-category chips that are never also correct, checked for plausibility in the slot. English contractions are natural but never a recall answer or speak token. French cloze segments keep the space before ? and !. British spellings stay banned. The capstone mix is a path-level property. Trophies already taken that drafts will reach for: Spanish todavía, entonces, cita, arreglar, funciona, luego, quizás; French hier, alors, vraiment, depuis, déjà, ensuite; English yet, still, just, then, already, actually, instead, because, until, appointment, repair, refund. Reviewer failure modes to pre-empt: recommending tú or tu toward staff, fixing French participle agreement to one gender, flagging them-turns against learner rails, flattening informal lessons to formal, and cloze rewrites that break the concat identity.

## Coupling, budget and boundary

**Base editions.** The field collector walks every locale-keyed object, so dialogue base text and pattern rules are in the hash and the editions. New B1 content changes the language's hash; all twelve editions must be regenerated with `generate-guided-base-editions.ts` and published together. That generator calls OpenRouter, and the local key is dead per STATE, so the owner must replace it before Milestone 3.

**Catalog.** The phrase catalog holds one row per lesson and vibe with the core phrase and its en and de translations; German B1 rows are already in it. Three new tiers add 300 rows through a new catalog migration, a locale patch and rollback tests, applied by the owner.

**TTS.** The scripts need only the slug tables. The per-language B1 cap fits German's measured total; the run-total cap means one language per invocation. Rotation continues each roster at offset 20: English P1 Serafina, Spanish P1 Lia, French P1 Lilly.

**Out of learner production, blanks, patterns and trophies**, allowed sparingly in them-turns: Spanish imperfecto de subjuntivo and si pudiera, aunque with subjunctive, pluscuamperfecto, pasiva con ser, reported speech with tense shift, condicional compuesto. French bien que and quoique, subjonctif passé, plus-que-parfait, conditionnel passé, concordance in reported speech, se faire + infinitif, gérondif, passé simple. English third and mixed conditionals, past perfect, would for past habits, backshifted reported speech, have or get something done, wish + past perfect, inversion. Register play, idiom and literary tenses stay out everywhere.

What the owner needs to decide, in one batch: the milestone shape with a held commit after P1 and one release bundle per language after P10; the French participle rule, voice-matched default or avoir-only; introducing tú and tu at B1; the English formal and casual semantics; a working OpenRouter key before editions. Later, per language: the catalog SQL and the TTS budget once a dry run measures the cost.
