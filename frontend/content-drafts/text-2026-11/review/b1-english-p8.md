1–4: Plausible gym-free everyday stories with genuine temporal contrast. However several literal form cues hand the learner the exact answer: “use nach didn't”, “look nach used to”, “walk im simple present mit I”, “get nach don't”, “buy nach used to”, “carry nach could”. Keep the operation in German, remove the solved token where appropriate. A first-person regular simple-present verb is trivially unchanged if the full lemma is given. Consider a more useful inflected slot. Standard US written “didn't use to” is coherent; don't imply an audible d distinction.

5: German “Die Fahrt dauert kürzer als …” is less idiomatic than “Die Fahrt dauert nicht so lange wie …”. Confirm the cue makes “takes” unique versus “took”; preserve meaningful time comparison. 6: Causal since is licensed by the fixed reserved trophy before duration is introduced. The German and English rules should make that contrast explicit. The loud/remember cues currently give words away again. 7: A little alarm clock beside the coffee maker does not explain how a learner can leave the phone outside the bedroom and still wake up to that clock. Put the alarm clock where it is used, or give it a coherent time-of-day function. 8–10: Focus and turns fit the newer architecture but directly answer-printing cues recur (keep/ask, use/change, leave); all P8 transfer examples differ from the hidden dialogue[3]. A few implausible chips (“inside the receipt”, “onto a recipe”) lower the challenge; choose plausible but contextually excluded alternatives without opening multiple solutions.
