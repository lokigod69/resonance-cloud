Both need revision before acceptance. Reapply these observations to the later bytes rather than undoing legitimate Fable corrections.

- P3 L1: 일곱 시 이후에 자리가 나면 can specify when the seat becomes available; the German reply specifies a seat in a class scheduled after seven. Make the same condition explicit in all three languages; the opening concerns class time.
- P3 L1–10: every single connector is the identical lead-in 그러면. Even if the native authority requires conditionals, ten identical connective tasks with repeatedly recycled enumeration/contrast distractors train a recognition shortcut. Use varied contextual connective practice or substantiate the narrow staging; retain required allocation and natural conditionals.
- P3 L2/3/4 and 7/8/9: the cheaper contract, desk closing time, table availability, one-week limit, 10% discount and exchange-only response support a concrete reply. Keep those grounding facts intact. P3 L5: the choice of the learner's work phone is private and not established; using purportedly non-SMS devices as options is not a good test of the conditional/request. P3 L6: immediate repetition of the staff's locker-key offer is intelligible; keep the time constraint as the point. P3 L10: morning vs afternoon call is a new learner preference, not a fact the interlocutor or scene establishes. Ensure choices do not grade private information and the requested call remains *before* the visit. P3 L5/9/10: step-3 pattern examples currently reveal the follow-up.
- P4 L1: an explicit either/or question (window or hallway) does not establish either as the correct answer, and initial 네 sounds like a yes/no reply. P4 L2: a pencil case, umbrella or swimsuit can all be in a bag. P4 L4: a desire to go early doesn't establish the business's actual opening time. P4 L9: a visit to a café, bookstore or pharmacy all work. Change what the exercise tests or supply disambiguating conversational evidence without leaking the learner's full answer.
- P4 L3 and L8: German floor convention differs from Korean. The draft renders 1층 as Erdgeschoss in L8 but 3층 as dritter Stock in L3. When the numbers refer to the same physical floor, German conventions place the Korean third floor on the second upper storey. Choose culturally unambiguous translations for these numbered floors rather than blindly matching digits.
- P4 L5–8: counting won, people, clock time and floors often gives an answer on units alone. Some counters are useful; do not let the noun-modifier practice collapse into ten unrelated guessing tasks. P4 L10: the other options describe shoes, bag or glasses despite a jacket already specified. Distinguish a supported jacket description from a learner asked to invent the color. No blocker asserted solely from color in L10.

Check both-base cues, coupled exercises/turns, opener and early pattern/situation after edits. No change to runtime, TTS, existing Korean A1/A2, or paid scope.
