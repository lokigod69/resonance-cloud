# Worlds — a story you can explore

The guided course teaches useful language step by step. Worlds gives that language
somewhere new to live: short original stories that reward reading, looking and
listening. A learner chooses a world because it interests them, not because a
streak or an exam demands it.

The distinctive unit is a **page with two depths**. Three connected story lines
move the narrative forward. Two details open optional language discoveries.
Explanations help without replacing the target text. Each story has an ending;
the experience is neither an endless feed nor another sequence of dialogue drills.

## First collection

Six worlds support sixteen stories, eight each in British English and European
Spanish, with two stories at each editorial level A1, A2, B1 and B2. The Spanish
stories have their own premises. They are not translated English episodes.
Talking animals and unusual events are explicitly fictional. Real cultural or
scientific articles will need primary sources and specialist review separately.

A1 uses concrete scenes and short present-tense language. A2 adds connected past
events and reasons. B1 brings hypotheses, perspective and reported speech. B2 adds
subtext, concession and register shifts. Longer sentences alone do not make a
story more advanced. These are editorial targets, not formal level certification.

## First usable layer

- Choose a language, level and story; see the full collection without artificial locks.
- Read six connected pages, explore the scene details and open meanings/notes.
- Toggle explanations in English or German in the study; preserve the chosen phrase.
- Resume a page locally, restart, revisit any page or open another story.
- Hear exact reviewed recordings when available. A missing recording is stated
  honestly; browser speech is not passed off as an ElevenLabs recording.

## Content and asset boundaries

Target text, translations, language notes, art briefs and speech coordinates are
separate. The audio list includes story/page titles, narrative/dialogue lines,
new-word labels, object labels and object descriptions. Art can change without
rewriting speech; speech changes invalidate its source-bound recording plan.
One narrator per language limits the first audition. Named speaker roles remain
available for later casting.

## Expansion after this collection

The same page structure can support original Japanese and Korean episodes with
comic composition and script-specific interaction. Those are future authored
editions, not automatic translations. Script pronunciation requires its own
expert review, segmentation and recordings. Other languages follow the same
editorial and accent checks. More A1 stories, richer cultural non-fiction and
additional worlds can extend the catalogue without changing the reader contract.

Later practice may hide a chosen phrase, ask for a description, or offer recall
from a scene. It should use the existing approved text/audio coordinates and
explicitly distinguish reading from an assessed attempt. No such assessment is
implemented or claimed in this study.
